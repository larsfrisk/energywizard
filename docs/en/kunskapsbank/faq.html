Frequently asked questions

FAQ

How do you account for solar irradiance?

Cloud cover from the weather forecast (cloud_cover_pct) reduces the modeled production by up to 85% compared to a clear sky - the more clouds, the lower the forecast. It's the same principle as a sunny day giving full output and an overcast day giving a fraction of it, just expressed as a formula instead of a guess.

How do you build the solar surplus forecast?

The base model is a clear-sky model: a sine curve between sunrise and sunset for the current month, scaled by a monthly efficiency factor and the system's peak output - the same principle as June producing more than December. But as soon as the house has built up its own history, a digital twin takes over: a learned profile of what the roof actually tends to produce per month and hour, built from the house's own logs instead of a generic formula. The longer the system runs, the more accurate the forecast becomes for your specific roof.

Can you choose your own priorities, or is it all fixed?

The order between the different control rules is fixed - it's built so that safety and economics always run in a defined, predictable order, not something every user should have to reason through. What you control are the parameters within that order:

  • Comfort temperature during cheap vs. expensive electricity (two separate setpoints)
  • Minimum level and time for each EV (e.g. at least 50% by 07:00)
  • Manual pause or forced charging, if you want to take over temporarily

That lets you tune comfort against savings without needing to understand or risk disturbing the whole decision logic.

Do I need solar panels or an EV for the system to be useful?

No. A heat pump alone is enough to get started - the heat control (the thermal battery) works regardless. Solar, an EV and a battery add more things the system can optimize against, but aren't a requirement.

How do you make sure the house doesn't get cold while the system saves money?

Price optimization must never come at the cost of basic comfort - minimum levels (for both indoor temperature and EV charging) are a hard limit in the control logic, not a goal the system tries to balance against. The system optimizes within that limit, never by lowering it.

Does everything run locally, or is the cloud needed?

EnergyWizard runs in your own Home Assistant (add-on) or in Docker. No account, no subscription and no cloud is needed to control anything. Devices Home Assistant talks to locally (heat pump, inverter, battery, charger over Modbus or a local API) are controlled locally. The internet is used for electricity prices, the weather forecast and devices that only have a cloud API, such as some cars and chargers. If the internet drops, EnergyWizard keeps running on the last plan.

What is sent to the developer (telemetry)?

Telemetry is always on in EnergyWizard's own builds and has no off switch. The code is free under AGPL-3.0, so you may modify it, including removing the telemetry, and distribute the modified version under the license. Once a day it sends: a random, hashed installation id, which equipment is used (driver, brand and model), approximate sizes, settings, rounded temperatures and error types (never the error message). Never names, addresses, IP addresses or measurement history. Exactly what is sent is in the README under "Telemetry".

Does EnergyWizard wear out the inverter's flash or Home Assistant's SD card?

No, it is built to avoid that. The export limit is written at most every five minutes, heat pump registers only when they change, and mode selects and power setpoints are not written if they already have the right value. Battery power is not rewritten until it changes by at least 200 W or 10%. In the add-on, the database batches its writes instead of forcing every row out to the SD card.

Can notifications go only to whoever is at home?

Yes. Settings → Notifications has "Only to whoever is home". House notifications then go to the phones whose person is at home according to Home Assistant (person.*). If nobody is home they go to every person's phone.

Can I see my own devices in "What is drawing power"?

Yes. Add devices with a power sensor (e.g. smart plugs on a washing machine or sauna) under loads: in the installation. They show as their own rows and are subtracted from the unexplained load. See the README.

The car charges slower than planned. Why?

If the car draws clearly less current than EnergyWizard ordered for five minutes, an explanation appears at the top of the app and the web view. Usually it is the charger's load balancing (Easee Equalizer or the circuit limit) cutting the current while the rest of the house draws a lot, the charger's max limit, or the car itself (onboard charger, charge limit, cold battery). Easee's current-limit sensors are disabled by default in Home Assistant. Enable them and EnergyWizard can tell you exactly which.

How do I see that EnergyWizard actually did what it said?

History has "Did we keep to the plan?": per device, the share of minutes the EV, heat pump and home battery did what was ordered, today or yesterday, and when they deviated. Notifications (app) and Aviseringar (web) list the sent notifications and how it went: sent, failed with a reason, category turned off or no recipient.
